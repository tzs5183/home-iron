HOME IRON

Built around your incline bench, dumbbells, bands (with a lat bar, V-bar, flat bar, tricep bar & ankle attachments), pole anchor and incline treadmill. The priority is bigger legs, balanced upper-body strength and better mobility — with two focused lower-body days, a dedicated mobility/cardio day, short daily flexibility work and enough calf training to grow without letting calves dominate the program.

Tap a plate to open that day — plates are color-coded like real bumper plates, heaviest (red) to lightest (white), with rest as the small steel collar.

Why this split works

The lower-body work is split into a quad-focused day and a posterior-chain day so you can push leg volume without cramming every squat, lunge and hinge into one workout. Similar movements have been trimmed back: push day uses flat + incline pressing instead of another standing chest press, Legs A uses squat + split-squat patterns without redundant lunges, and Legs B adds direct hamstring curls using your ankle attachments. Calves get three direct sessions per rotation — heavy straight-leg work on Legs A, moderate single-leg work on Pull, and bent-knee soleus work on Legs B — while incline walking provides additional indirect work.

How to run this week

  • Warm-up is now the first item on every lifting day — 5 easy minutes on the treadmill, then a light warm-up set of that day's first exercise before you load up.
  • Progression: once you hit the top of a rep range for all sets, add weight (or move to a heavier band) next session and drop back to the bottom of the range.
  • Rest between sets: 60–90s for isolation moves (raises, curls, calves), 90–120s for the bigger lifts (squats, presses, deadlifts, rows).
  • Fat loss comes mostly from your overall calorie balance — this plan supplies the training side (muscle-building lifts + incline cardio); pair it with a modest calorie deficit and enough protein (roughly 0.7–1g per lb of bodyweight) to protect the muscle you're building while you lose fat.
  • Heavy bands: if you're running your 160lb bands pre-tensioned, confirm your pole anchor is rated for that force (not just the band). Keep face pulls and tricep pushdowns on a moderate band regardless of how strong you get elsewhere — they load your shoulder near your head/face at end range.
  • Traps & core: traps get direct work from shrugs on pull day plus indirect work from rows and RDLs. Core work is spread across the lifting days using different patterns rather than repeating several similar ab exercises in one session.
  • Flexibility now runs daily — a short 5–8 minute stretch after every lifting day, plus the full deep-stretch flow on the mobility day. Tight hamstrings and hips (what's behind not touching your toes, and a lot of golf-swing stiffness) respond much better to short, frequent stretching than one long session a week — expect gradual, steady change over weeks, not one big breakthrough session.
